## Context

What situation, constraint, or force is driving this decision? Describe the
problem, the relevant requirements, and any prior decisions this depends on.
State enough context that a reader who was not present understands *why a
decision is needed now*.

## Options considered

List the realistic alternatives, each with a one-line characterization of its
main trade-off. Include the option that was ultimately chosen and the ones that
were rejected — the rejected options are as important as the winner.

- **Option A — …** — trade-off.

## Decision

State the decision in a single, unambiguous sentence or two. Name the chosen
option explicitly.

## Rationale

Explain *why* the chosen option beats the alternatives for this project, tying
back to the context and requirements. This is the heart of the ADR.

## Positive consequences

The benefits gained. Be specific and honest.

## Negative consequences

The costs accepted. Every real decision has them; naming them is what makes the
record credible.

## Risks

What could go wrong, what assumptions this rests on, and how they are mitigated
or monitored.

## Conditions that would justify revisiting

The concrete signals — new requirements, scale thresholds, pain points — that
would trigger a new ADR to supersede this one.
